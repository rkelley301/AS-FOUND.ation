PID Loop Control

01 Why This Matters Now
⚡ Hook Every process loop you touch in the field — temperature, pressure, flow, level — is probably being controlled by a PID, and if you can't read what the three terms are doing, you're flying blind when it starts hunting, oscillating, or drifting off setpoint.

03 Core Concept — 3 Points Only
P
Proportional
Reacts to size of error
Too low Kp = lazy. Too high = oscillation.
I
Integral
Kills steady-state offset
Too much Ki = windup, overshoot, instability.
D
Derivative
Brakes before overshoot
Too much Kd = amplifies noise. Often left at 0.
  • Error (e) = SP − PV. This is what the controller is always reacting to. If error is zero, P and D output nothing — only I can still produce output.
  • Integral windup is your #1 field problem. When output saturates (valve fully open) and error persists, I keeps accumulating. Loop goes unstable when demand drops. Look for anti-windup in your controller config.
  • Tuning direction matters. In most DCS/PLC platforms, you tune Gain (Kp), Integral time (Ti), and Derivative time (Td) — not raw gains. Higher Ti = less integral action. Know your platform's convention before touching anything.
04 Hands-On Exercise

Tune a Simulated Loop — Right Now

  • Go to pidtuner.com or search "PID simulator online." FREE
    Alternative: Google "PID loop simulator" — Incubit or similar works.
  • Set Kp=1, Ki=0, Kd=0. Apply a setpoint step. Watch the process variable climb — does it reach SP or stop short? That gap is steady-state offset — P alone can't fix it.
  • Now add Ki=0.1. Watch offset disappear. Crank it to Ki=5 and watch it overshoot and oscillate. You just caused integral windup.
  • Reset to a stable Ki. Add Kd=0.5. The approach to setpoint should smooth out. Increase Kd until it gets twitchy — that's derivative amplifying noise.
  • Write down (on paper, not mental note) the Kp/Ki/Kd combo that gave you the fastest stable response with no overshoot. That's your baseline intuition for a first-order process.

06 Cheat Sheet Card
PID Quick Reference — Pocket Card

Term Behavior

  • P too high →Oscillation
  • P too low →Sluggish, won't reach SP
  • I too high →Windup / overshoot
  • I too low →Steady-state offset
  • D too high →Noise amplification
  • D = 0 →Usually fine for most loops

Field Symptoms → Fix

  • PV won't hit SP →↑ Ki
  • Overshoot + oscillate →↓ Ki, check windup
  • Slow response →↑ Kp
  • Loop hunts →↓ Kp first
  • Noisy output →↓ Kd or filter PV
  • Windup suspected →Check anti-windup setting

Key Terms

  • SPSetpoint (target)
  • PVProcess Variable (actual)
  • CV / OutputController output to valve/actuator
  • Error (e)SP − PV
  • WindupI accumulating during saturation
  • OffsetPersistent error P can't fix

Tuning Order (Field Rule)

  • Step 1Tune P first. Stable?
  • Step 2Add I until offset gone
  • Step 3Add D only if needed
  • AlwaysMake one change at a time
  • AlwaysDocument before/after values
  • NeverTune in AUTO during upset
